AC Leak Water Damage Help in Tyler

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. Our team measures how far it traveled, dries the building materials and fixes the damage. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the System DownSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. Where the wiring on the air handler is getting wet, the breaker should also go off.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Call Both TradesAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Leave the system off until it is fixed.
Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. Repairing the air conditioner itself is typically not covered either way. Read your policy, and ask us for photos and moisture readings to support the claim.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Measure the Spread

With thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks the unit, the adjacent walls or ceiling and the floor beyond the closet, so wet materials are found even where no stain shows.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

Standing water in the pan, the platform and the carpet is extracted. Drywall or insulation that is saturated or moldy is cut out so the cavity behind it can air out.

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, usually 3 to 5, while we track the readings. We place equipment so the HVAC technician can still reach the unit.

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, because condensate that has sat for days is rarely clean. New flooring, baseboard, drywall and insulation follow, and fresh paint makes the area match.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • A rusted, cracked or out of level drain pan that lets water escape.
  • Low refrigerant or a clogged filter, either of which leads to a frozen coil.
  • More meltwater than the pan can handle after an iced-over coil thaws.
  • Sweating ducts and pipes in a humid attic wetting the drywall under them.
  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.

Where the Damage Spreads

Floors Outside the Closet

Carpet pad, laminate and hardwood near the closet all get wet from underneath as the leak spreads past the door. Hardwood cups when it is not dried, while laminate swells for good.

Drywall Below the Air Handler

An attic air handler leaks onto the drywall beneath it. The ceiling stains first, then sags, and a section soaked for days can fall into the hallway or bedroom below.

Mold Around the Air Handler

Mold may appear in 24 to 48 hours on damp materials by the air handler, where the return air stream can carry its spores through the ductwork to other rooms.

Attic Insulation

Batts or blown-in material next to the pan absorb the leak and press that moisture onto the ceiling. Wet insulation loses its value and often has to be replaced.
