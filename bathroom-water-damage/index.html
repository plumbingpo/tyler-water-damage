Bathroom Water Damage in Tyler

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. In Tyler, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Warning Signs

  • Vinyl flooring that curls at the edges or has discolored near the toilet or tub
  • A hollow sound, movement, or loose tiles in the floor by the bathtub
  • Cracked, peeling or mold blackened caulk where the tub or shower meets the wall
  • A warped or swollen cabinet floor under the vanity sink
  • Directly under the bathroom, a ceiling with a brown stain or a sag
  • Dark stains at the base of the toilet, or a bowl that wobbles a little
What We Do

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Pinpoint the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Cavities

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Seal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Slow Vanity Drips

Months can pass with a supply valve or drain joint dripping, hidden by whatever is stored in the vanity. The cabinet floor swells, and the water runs through to the subfloor and the wall behind it.

Trapped Steam

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.

The Room Below

The stain on the lower ceiling can be several feet off from the leak itself, because water travels along pipes and joists first. Wiring and light fixtures may lie along that route.

Mold in the Tiled Wall

By themselves, grout and tile do not keep water out. Mold takes hold on the wall board once that hidden layer remains wet, and no scrubbing can get to it.

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

The first few days

Water Slips Through

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

The first several weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Between showers, the wood below the tile has no time to dry out. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

Months one to three

Signs Start to Appear

You see darker grout, loosening tiles and blackened caulk. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.
