Crawl Space Water Removal Help in Tyler

Few homeowners notice right away when water collects beneath the floor. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. We pump the space dry, remove the ruined material and get the wood dried while it is still sound.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. Finding the cause matters just as much, since a dripping water line, a failed drain or groundwater from a storm will flood the area again unless it is addressed.

What It Looks Like

  • An odor like damp soil that gets stronger close to the registers
  • Puddles or wet muck visible just inside the crawl space door
  • Ducts and plumbing lines below the house covered in condensation
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
  • Edges of the floorboards lifting into a washboard pattern in the rooms above
  • An increase in insects and pests that like damp, dark spaces
What We Do

How We Handle Crawl Space Water Removal

1

Safety Check and Inspection

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Where mold has begun or is likely, the bare joists and subfloor get cleaned and an antimicrobial applied.

4

Dry and Verify

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Air From Below

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

Pests Drawn to Moisture

A wet crawl space offers the soft wood and moisture that wood destroying insects and mice prefer. Once the area is dry it becomes far less inviting.

Weakened Floor Framing

The first soft wood is usually hidden, up where joists meet the subfloor or back at the sill plate. Our crew tests the lumber by hand to locate weak areas.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Under a typical house you will find cable runs, electrical boxes and duct lines. When duct insulation is saturated it sends stale air around the home, while flooded wiring creates a real risk of shock.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First day

Water Pools Below

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. Soaked insulation gains weight and starts to sag off the floor above.

Days 3 through 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. The lumber goes on taking in moisture, a stale smell spreads and flooring can begin to cup.

Past one week

Wood Starts Rotting

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Joists and sill plates soften, fasteners rust and the damp space attracts termites and other pests.
