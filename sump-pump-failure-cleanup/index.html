Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Help in Tyler

The moment a basement most needs its sump pump is often the moment the pump gives out. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. From there it tops the edge of the basin and runs out over the slab. Nothing stops it until some pump is working again.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Keep Out of the BasementDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. Outlets, the pump cord or the furnace might already be wet and live.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerTurn off the basement circuits at the breaker panel, but only if you can reach it from a dry spot. A panel surrounded by water is a job for the power company.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceLook to see whether the pump came unplugged, its outlet tripped or the float is pinned against the pit wall. Keep your hands out of the water.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.
Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Loss of power at the pump alone, from a loose cord or a tripped circuit.
  • Dirt and small rocks washed into the basin that lock up the pump.
  • A discharge line that is blocked, crushed or disconnected outside the house.
  • Years of on and off cycling finally burning out an aging motor.
  • More water flowing into the pit than an undersized pump can move.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Basement Floor Coverings

Groundwater-soaked carpet and padding come out, as cleaning cannot make them safe. Silty water also gets caught below vinyl plank and laminate, which normally means lifting them.

Furnace and Water Heater

Sump pits tend to be located near the home's mechanical equipment. If wiring, burners or control boards got wet they may be ruined, and no appliance should be restarted until it has been checked.

Low Electrical Outlets

Wall outlets near the floor are sometimes submerged in a sump flood. Have an electrician look at wet receptacles and cable before power is restored to them.

Bottom Plates and Studs

The wood plate at the base of each wall sits right on the slab and soaks up water first. We clean, disinfect and dry the studs and plates after removing the wet drywall.

Insurance

Insurance and Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. Water backing up or overflowing from a sump is usually excluded under a standard homeowners policy, and a flood policy is a different product with rules of its own. Unless you bought an endorsement for sump overflow or water backup there is often no coverage, and such endorsements are capped. Check the declarations page for it, or call your agent to find out. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • How deep the water got and how much of the basement it covered.
  • How many stored belongings have to be moved, cleaned or thrown away.
  • How long the water stood before pumping and drying could begin.
  • If water is still coming in, the cost of holding it back with a temporary pump.

How We Fix It

Our Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Process

1

Get the Water Out

Because the pumps and extraction units are ours, the broken sump does not hold up the work. Standing water and silt are removed from the floor, the pit area and any low spots.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Clean and Sanitize

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. Drying comes afterward, because fans on a dirty basement would blow contaminants everywhere.

4

Dry and Confirm

Dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture meter readings on the framing and concrete return to normal, typically 3 to 5 days. A working pump should be back in the pit before our equipment leaves.
