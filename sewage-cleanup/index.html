Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. Across Tyler, Tyler Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Address slow drains promptly, before they become complete blockages
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Roots from trees close to the sewer line work their way into pipes and block them, so have them cleared
  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
