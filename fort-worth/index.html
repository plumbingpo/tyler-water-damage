Water Damage We See Most in Fort Worth

Water usually escapes at the same few vulnerable spots in a home. These are the ones Fort Worth property owners report to us again and again.

Water heaters: Sediment collects in the bottom of a tank and slowly eats through the steel. Look for moisture at the base, rust streaks on the jacket or a small pool that keeps coming back. If the tank ruptures, it keeps refilling and draining onto the floor until someone closes the cold supply valve. When the unit lives in an attic or interior closet, the water reaches carpets and ceilings far from it.

Roofs: One strong storm is enough to peel back shingles and push water beneath the roof covering. Skylights, plumbing vents and chimneys are other entry points once their seals crack with age. Water travels along rafters before it drips, so the wet spot inside may be far from the hole outside. For roof leaks in Fort Worth, we tarp where needed, pull out soaked insulation and dry the structure so the ceiling can be repaired.

Freezing: Depending on the season, a hard overnight freeze can burst lines that were fine the day before. The first to go are hose bibs, irrigation lines and any pipe routed through an unheated space. Vacant homes and rooms with the heat turned down are especially exposed. If a faucet only trickles on a freezing morning, shut off the water and call before the pipe thaws.

Backups: A drain that gurgles or several fixtures that slow down together point to a blockage in the main line. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. Since the waste is hazardous, we extract it and then sanitize concrete, tile and other hard surfaces. Stay out of the affected rooms and avoid running water until the line is cleared.

Seepage: Dampness at the base of a basement or foundation wall often means water is collecting outside. The usual reasons are grading that tilts inward, clogged perimeter drains and downspouts that empty too close. White chalky deposits, peeling masonry paint and a persistent damp smell are early warnings.
