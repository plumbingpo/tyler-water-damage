Few water damage events are as common, or as potentially devastating to a property, as a pipe that bursts. One broken line can put out 4 to 8 gallons every minute, so 240 to 480 gallons an hour may be running through floors, ceilings and walls. Whether the cause is freezing temperatures, aging plumbing, excessive water pressure, corrosion or physical damage, a burst pipe demands immediate emergency response to limit the damage water does to structural materials, flooring, personal belongings and building systems.

Tyler Restoration provides 24/7 emergency response for burst pipe water damage throughout Tyler and surrounding areas. Technicians on our team assist with finding and closing the water supply, while licensed plumbers we coordinate with handle a proper plumbing repair. As soon as the flow is shut down, extraction starts: portable and truck-mounted units pull standing water out of every affected area as fast as we can.

Following extraction, our structural drying process addresses all moisture-affected materials, including drywall, insulation, wood framing, subflooring and finished flooring. Since water runs downhill through ceiling spaces, floor assemblies and wall cavities, a burst pipe frequently affects several rooms on more than one level. Our thermal imaging and moisture detection equipment traces water migration paths so every affected area is identified and properly dried, which prevents the mold growth and structural deterioration that hidden moisture causes.

How to Tell You Need Burst Pipe Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • An unusually high water bill that points to a hidden leak
  • A sudden drop in water pressure throughout your property
  • Stale or earthy smells coming from a cabinet or wall cavity
  • Water pooling on floors with no apparent source
  • Visible water spraying or dripping from exposed pipes
  • Faucets that give only a trickle, or nothing, during a freeze
  • Brownish water at the faucet, which can mean corroded pipes

How to Prevent Burst Pipe Repair Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Outdoor faucets should be closed, drained and free of hoses ahead of freezing weather
  • Going away in cold months? Keep the thermostat running to protect the plumbing from freezing
  • Unheated spaces like garages, attics and crawl spaces need pipe insulation on exposed lines
  • Know where your main water shutoff valve is and how to operate it quickly
  • Replace old galvanized steel or polybutylene pipes before they fail unexpectedly
  • Put leak sensors by the water heater and along the main supply line
