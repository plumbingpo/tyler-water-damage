Preventing Water Heater Floods in Tyler, TX

Home / Blog / Preventing Water Heater Floods in Tyler, TX

Water heater failures are one of the most common causes of residential water damage in Tyler, TX. It takes only minutes for a ruptured 40 to 50 gallon unit, the standard size, to empty completely into the house, leaving stored belongings, walls, and floors with damage worth thousands of dollars. If you know the warning signs and keep up with prevention, you can avoid this costly emergency.

Signs Your Water Heater Is About to Fail

Several clues point to a tank on its way out, including an age beyond 8 to 12 years (the normal life of a tank-style heater), a relief valve that frequently discharges or drips, less hot water than before, dampness or pooling water near the bottom of the unit, corrosion or rust you can see on the tank, popping or rumbling noises in heating cycles, and rusty-looking water at the hot taps.

Preventive Maintenance

Regular maintenance extends water heater life and reduces failure risk: flush the tank annually to remove sediment buildup. Every 2 to 3 years, inspect the anode rod, and put in a new one if corrosion is significant. The pressure and temperature relief valve should be tested every year. Look over the supply connections for signs of wear or corrosion. Keep the area around the water heater clear for inspection access. You may also want a water leak sensor installed close to the bottom of the tank.

When to Replace Your Water Heater

The most effective way to prevent a catastrophic water heater failure is proactive replacement before the unit fails. A heater that is more than 10 years old, shows corrosion, or keeps needing repairs should be replaced now, before it floods. The cost of a planned replacement is far less than the cost of emergency water damage restoration. Going tankless is worth a look, since it removes the risk of a tank failure altogether.

What to Do If Your Water Heater Floods

First, stop water going into the failed tank right away by closing the cold inlet valve on top; after that, shut the main water valve too as a backup.
