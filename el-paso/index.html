Drying Out an El Paso Property: What to Expect

Drying a building is not the same as waiting for it to dry. Left alone, water trapped in drywall, wood framing and subfloor can stay for weeks, which is long enough for mold and warping. On each El Paso job, our task is to make that happen quickly and under control.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Next, air movers placed around the room drive air over damp materials so the water in them evaporates. Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air and send it down a drain, which keeps the room dry enough for evaporation to continue. Air movers on their own only stir humid air, so the two machines work as a pair.

A technician measures the wet materials daily against readings from an unaffected area of the property. For most El Paso homes the target is met in 3 to 5 days, though hardwood, plaster and other dense materials need more time. Restoration Challenges El Paso Homeowners Face

Walk through a house from the roof down and you pass most of the places a leak can begin. That is the route we take when inspecting a property in El Paso, and five spots along it account for most calls.

Attics: Rain that slips past a damaged shingle or a loose vent boot lands on the attic floor first. Because insulation absorbs so much, a leak overhead may run through several storms before a mark appears below. Wet insulation stops insulating and keeps the wood beneath it damp. Our part is removing what is saturated and drying the wood, while a roofing contractor closes the opening.

Bathrooms: A tub left filling, a sink with a blocked overflow hole or a toilet that runs over can soak a bathroom floor quickly. Tile looks waterproof, yet water gets through grout lines and around the edges of the room. In an upstairs bathroom the next stop is the ceiling of the room underneath. Anything that came out of the toilet bowl should be treated as dirty water and cleaned with disinfectant.

Walls: Plumbing hidden in a wall may seep at a joint for weeks without leaving a puddle anywhere. The hints are small: a baseboard that swells, a stain that grows, or a patch of wall that feels cool and soft. On these calls in El Paso, an infrared camera and a pin meter show us how far the dampness reaches. After the pipe is repaired by a plumber, we open just the damp part and dry the cavity.

Tanks: The hot water tank sits out of sight in a garage, closet or utility space and stays full all the time. Worn parts announce themselves with moisture at the top connections, the drain spout or the pressure relief pipe. When the tank itself fails, close the cold water valve above it and switch off the gas or power to the unit. Our Tyler Restoration crew extracts the water and checks the nearby walls and flooring, which often get wet from behind.

Drains: A clogged sewer line makes itself known at whichever drain sits lowest. If murky water comes up in a downstairs shower or floor drain, waste has nowhere to go but back inside.
